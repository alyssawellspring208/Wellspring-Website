The women's health gap starts earlier than you think

October 28, 2026. 4 min read

By WellSpring Health & Wellness

Women live longer than men on average, yet they spend more of those years in poor health. Much of that gap opens in midlife, and the signs often start in the mid-30s.

A gap measured in years

The McKinsey Health Institute found that women spend about 25% more of their lives in poor health than men, about nine years on average. Its researchers estimate that closing the gap could add at least $1 trillion a year to the global economy by 2040.

The institute traces part of the gap to research: the U.S. National Institutes of Health spends about 11% of its budget on research specific to women's health. It traces another part to how care is delivered.

Midlife symptoms often go untreated

More than 450 million women worldwide have menopause or perimenopause symptoms, and about 80% of them say the symptoms interfere with their lives. Fatigue, poor sleep, brain fog, weight changes, and mood shifts are common.

Even so, more than 80% of women don't seek medical care for menopause symptoms, according to studies PwC cites in its 2026 menopause report.

It starts in the mid-30s

In 2026, Boston Consulting Group surveyed 10,608 people in 12 countries about longevity. Hormonal shifts and fertility challenges, which arrive around an average age of 36 to 38, raised women's interest in long-term health more than menopause itself did.

Women in perimenopause and menopause also spend more: a median $84 a month on longevity products and services, compared with $68 for other women and $73 for men. Only about half of them say they are satisfied with what they get.

What earlier care looks like

Earlier care follows three habits. Measure first: a full evaluation with labs and body composition sets a baseline before symptoms pile up. Treat what the numbers show, under a licensed clinician. Then follow up over time and adjust the plan as your body changes.
