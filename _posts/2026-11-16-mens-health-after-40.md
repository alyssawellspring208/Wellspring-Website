Men often notice changes in their health during their 40s and 50s. They may feel more tired or take longer to recover after exercise. An annual checkup may also reveal changes in blood pressure or blood sugar. A clinician can help determine which findings deserve attention.

## Start with blood pressure and blood sugar

High blood pressure can develop without noticeable symptoms. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening adults for hypertension. It also recommends confirming an elevated reading with measurements outside the clinic before starting treatment.

The Task Force recommends screening adults ages 35 to 70 who have overweight or obesity for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes. Your clinician can consider your medical history and individual risk when deciding whether testing is appropriate.

## Make time for movement

Regular physical activity supports cardiovascular health and physical function. The CDC recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity each week, along with muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days.

Walking and resistance training can provide a starting point. The right routine depends on your health and physical abilities.

## Ask informed questions about testosterone

Low energy can have several causes. The Endocrine Society recommends diagnosing hypogonadism when a man has compatible symptoms and consistently low testosterone levels confirmed through appropriate testing. Its clinical guideline advises against routine testosterone screening in men without symptoms.

Discuss your concerns with a clinician who can evaluate your symptoms and determine whether hormone testing is appropriate.

## Find care that fits your needs

Health concerns vary from one person to another. At WellSpring, our goal is to understand each patient's health history and explain the care options available to them. A licensed clinician can recommend an evaluation or treatment based on individual needs.
